Anonymous usage data

In short

The Games Hub and its games (including the Driftworks apps for Android and iOS) send anonymous usage data to this site's own server, so we can see which games are played and where players get stuck, and make the games better. Nothing goes to advertisers, Google or any other company. There are no cookies, and no account is needed.

What is collected

  • A random id made by your browser or app the first time you play, kept on your device. It is not based on anything about you or your device, and it is not linked to a save code or an email address.
  • When you play: when a page opens, a heartbeat every few minutes while it is on screen, and how long you were away.
  • Your device, roughly: phone, tablet or desktop; operating system; browser; screen size rounded to 50 pixels; whether it has a touch screen; its language and time zone; and which site linked you here, if any.
  • What happens in the games: for example a round of Snake started and its score, or in Driftworks the goals you complete, research, ships built, missions, and choices in the menus. Save names, save codes and email addresses are never sent.
  • Errors: the first few script errors on a page, without personal details.

Your IP address is used to deliver the data, as with any web request, and to stop abuse with a short-lived rate limit kept in memory. It is not stored.

Why, and on what basis

To find out how the games are played and improve them: which parts are too slow or too hard, what nobody discovers, what breaks on which devices. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in improving the games (GDPR article 6(1)(f)). The data is only used for this, is looked at in totals, and is never sold, shared or used for advertising.

Where it is kept, and for how long

On the Games Hub's own servers in the European Union. Usage data is deleted automatically after 13 months.

Your choices

You can object at any time by turning usage data off: with the switch above on this site, or in Driftworks under Settings, Privacy (also in the apps). Turning it off sends one last note that it was turned off, then nothing more, and deletes the random id from your device. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, nothing is collected at all.

Because the id is random and stays on your device, we cannot tell which data is yours. That means we cannot look it up or delete it on request; turning usage data off is how you stop it. Turning it on again starts with a new id.

Cloud saves

Cloud saves are separate from usage data. A cloud save stores your game with its save code, which is kept only as a keyed hash. An email address you link for recovery is kept as a keyed hash too, and used only to send you your save codes. Deleting a save in the game deletes it from the server.

Questions

Open an issue on GitHub. You also have the right to complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.
